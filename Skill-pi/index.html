Skill-π

Skill-Policy Co-Evolution for Self-Improving Multimodal Reasoning

Pingyi Miao1, Xianlai Chen1, Yunbo Wang1,†, Yuxin Peng2

1 Central South University, 2 Peking University

† Corresponding author: Yunbo Wang

☷ Method▥ Results▧ Examples

Abstract

Post-training with explicit reasoning trajectories is important for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). However, obtaining high-quality reasoning trajectories typically requires costly human annotation or supervision from stronger external models. To reduce this cost, self-improvement paradigms have emerged, enabling models to generate, verify, and learn from their own reasoning experience. Despite their effectiveness, existing self-improvement methods remain largely trajectory-centric, repeatedly consuming sample-specific reasoning trajectories while leaving persistently unresolved samples with little contribution to subsequent improvement. In this work, we introduce Skill-π, a skill-policy co-evolution framework that turns self-generated multimodal experience into reusable reasoning skills and continuously expands their capability coverage. In Skill-π, a shared MLLM policy jointly performs skill selection, utilization, and evolution, with stage-specific credit assignment yielding learning signals according to the downstream consequences of each decision. The newly-recovered successful experience further improves the parametric policy and evolves the skill library, allowing reusable skills and the policy to improve iteratively across evolution rounds. Experiments show that Skill-π achieves up to 3× higher evolutionary efficiency and 2.7× greater data efficiency than existing self-improvement methods. Across diverse benchmarks spanning mathematical, chart/document, and general multimodal reasoning, Skill-π also achieves stronger performance against representative multimodal reasoning models.

Skill-π

Overview

Skill-π is a skill-policy co-evolution framework for self-improving multimodal reasoning. It preserves self-generated experience in both the parametric policy and a persistent library of reusable reasoning skills. Successful trajectories improve the policy, while recurring reasoning behaviors across related successes are abstracted into transferable strategies.

Skill Construction and Reuse

Verified successful samples are grouped by visual-state compatibility and question similarity. Recurring reasoning strategies are consolidated into skills. For a new sample, the shared policy retrieves compatible skills, selects distinct strategies, and generates multiple reasoning trajectories under each selected skill. Verified successes provide new experience for subsequent improvement.

Stage-Specific Credit Assignment

Skill selection receives credit from downstream outcomes across skill branches. Utilization receives credit by comparing trajectories within the same branch. Evolution proposals receive delayed credit through subsequent reuse of the constructed skills. Stage-masked GRPO applies each learning signal to the corresponding decision stage, allowing the policy and skill library to improve together.

Experiment Results

Main Results

We evaluate Skill-π-7B and Skill-π-8B, based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B, across mathematical, chart/document, and general multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

Skill-π-7B improves average accuracy from 58.3 to 65.5, a gain of 7.2 percentage points over the base model. Skill-π-8B improves the average from 70.3 to 75.4, a gain of 5.1 points. Improvements extend across all seven benchmarks.

Comparison with Self-Improvement Methods

For a controlled comparison with self-improvement methods, we use the SFT-only variant, Skill-π-SFT, under the same Qwen2.5-VL backbones and training protocol.

Iterative Self-Improvement

The 7B model improves on every benchmark across three co-evolution rounds. Average accuracy increases from 60.1 after cold-start initialization to 62.0, 63.8, and 65.5, yielding a cumulative gain of 5.4 percentage points.

Case Study Examples

The following examples illustrate how retrieved skills supply the reasoning operation needed to turn perceived evidence into a consistent answer. Responses are condensed from the qualitative examples.

Reference-Level Comparison

Question. Which endpoint is farther from sea level?

Selected skill. Compare absolute distances from the reference level. Identify the two elevations, calculate their absolute values, and compare the resulting distances.

Base-model response. Selects the mountain peak because its elevation is positive.

Skill-assisted response. |−350| = 350 > |325| = 325. The underground lake is farther from sea level.

Reflection Geometry

Question. P₁ and P₂ are reflections of P across OA and OB. The segment P₁P₂ meets OA at M and OB at N. If P₁P₂ = 6 cm, what is the perimeter of △PMN?

Selected skill. Convert reflection symmetry into equal-length constraints: PM = P₁M and PN = P₂N.

Base-model response. Interprets symmetry as implying half of P₁P₂ and answers 3 cm.

Skill-assisted response. PM + MN + PN = P₁M + MN + NP₂ = P₁P₂ = 6 cm.
